# Faradworks: a skeleton

Not a draft you can publish. A shape you can write into, built only from things you have
already said, with the gaps marked. Fill the square brackets and it becomes a post.

Your own lines are placed where I think they belong. Move them.

---

## Title

Two candidates, both yours:

- **We raised $200K to automate PCB design. Then the market changed the question.**
- **The technology got better. The business case didn't.**

The first is the better headline because it sets up a turn. The second is the better last line.
Using both, one at each end, is probably right.

---

## 1. It started with a favour

You already have this beat, and it is on the rail: November 2018, someone from the electronics
department had the hardware and was missing a small piece of software, and you said you could do
it in a day. You swapped rooms the next semester.

> [How did it go from roommates to cofounders? What was the conversation, and roughly when?]
> [Why him, and why you? What did each of you bring that the other did not have?]

*This is the strongest opening available to you, and no other founder story on the internet has it.*

## 2. The bet

> [What exactly were you automating? Schematic capture, layout, component selection, review?
>  A reader who has never opened a PCB tool needs one sentence that makes the problem real.]
> [What was Pinscope? It is named in your own brief and nowhere else.]
> [What made you both believe it was possible in 2025 and not before?]

## 3. Generation, editing, verification

Your line: *"We moved from generation to editing to verification. The product got technically
stronger while the market kept narrowing."*

This is the spine of the whole piece. Three moves down the ladder of ambition, each one taken
because you learned something.

> [What forced the first move, from generating designs to editing them?]
> [What forced the second, from editing to verifying?]
> [At each step, what got better technically?]

## 4. What the market actually said

> [Who did you talk to? Hardware startups, contract manufacturers, large OEMs?]
> [What did they say they wanted, and what did they actually pay for today?]
> [What was the smallest customer who would have been worth having, and why was that hard?]

*"The market kept narrowing" is the claim. This section is the evidence for it, and without a
couple of real conversations in it, the sentence is just a shape.*

## 5. The money

$200K with Antler.

> [What did that buy: months of runway, headcount, hardware?]
> [What was expected of you in return, and on what clock?]

## 6. Why it stopped

Your line: *"The technology got better. The business case didn't."*

> [Was there a deciding moment, or did it thin out? Who said it first?]
> [What would have had to be true for it to keep going?]

*The honest version of this section is the reason anyone will trust the rest of the page. The
temptation is to blame timing. Resist it in proportion to how true it is.*

## 7. What you would tell someone starting the same thing

> [One paragraph. Not lessons-learned. What you would actually say to a founder who called you.]

## Notes on scope

Aim for 800 to 1200 words. The Optimus deep dive is long because the system was large and the
detail is the point. This one is short because the story is a decision, not an architecture.

Two things to decide while writing:

**Does your cofounder appear by name?** His call, not yours. The penname works if he prefers it.

**Does the LED matrix card hand forward, or does this reach back?** Only one of them should carry
the thread. My view: the LED matrix card keeps its line, because a reader meets it first and the
payoff is better as a surprise than as a callback.
